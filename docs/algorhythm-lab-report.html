Abstract

We describe Algorhythm, a design for game agents in which a person writes many interchangeable functions for each decision, and a population of Monte Carlo simulations chooses among them. Control flows through three layers, drawn by analogy with a frequency-modulation modular synthesizer: continuous weights act as analogue dials, whole-number enumerations act as rotary switches selecting exactly one function, and a final learned transform, which reads the agent's senses and the deterministic function's own answer, may override that answer when it is confident. The transform may be several steps, and at all zeros it leaves the rules unchanged. We report the design's evolution on a working fleet of nine machines, its implementation in the tank game treadcore, a new five-way pinging function as a worked example of A/B/C/D/E selection, and a biorhythm input. All of the new layers are silent by default, verified by identical results on nine scenario families. We have not yet finished training a transform or measured the new choices over a full census, and we report those as open. The closest prior work we found is Programming by Optimization and residual policy learning. We found no system combining all of these in this form, and our search was not exhaustive.

Index Terms: algorithm configuration, game AI, hybrid intelligence, Monte Carlo evaluation, programming by optimization, residual learning, sequential testing.

I. Introduction

Writing game AI usually forces a choice. Hand-written rules are legible and start out competent, but they stop improving when their author does. A learned network can improve without limit, but starts at nothing. In this fleet the difference is stark: the hand-written brain beats every learned model we have by about twenty points a seat.

Paul Richeson's proposal is to stop choosing, and to think of a program as a synthesizer. "Analogue dials (weights) ... used for between the provided functions. Some functions are like a switch dial on a synth making 0-7 of the sounds of that dial." The author supplies many of each component. Generally any combination should run, some work better than others, and as with FM sounds, certain patches make the sum greater than the whole. A Monte Carlo of simulated games is the ear that decides.

II. Prior Work

Programming by Optimization. The nearest work we found is Hoos's Programming by Optimization [1]. Its central idea is the one here: treat a program as a large space of programs, avoid committing prematurely to design choices, and let an optimization procedure choose. That work targets algorithm design generally, usually for runtime or solution quality on problem instances, rather than agents in an adversarial game.

Algorithm selection and configuration. Rice posed choosing an algorithm for a problem as a formal question in 1976 [2]. SMAC [3] extended automated configuration from few numerical parameters to many categorical ones, the same mix of dials and switches used here. Our search does not use SMAC's models; it uses a fit for dials and exhaustive A/B for switches.

Residual learning. Silver et al. [4] and Johannink et al. [5] improve a hand-designed controller by learning a correction added to its output, rather than learning from nothing. Our transform stage is this idea, with two additions: a confidence gate, and several steps.

Programmatic policies. Verma et al. [6] go in the opposite direction, searching for a program that imitates a trained network. It shares the belief that programs and networks should be combined.

Sequential testing in engines. Chess engines proved the methodology of testing every change on many games: Stockfish's Fishtest runs SPRT over distributed volunteers' machines [7]. Our sequential read and held-out reads are from that tradition. Population Based Training [8] jointly optimizes models and hyperparameters in a population, and is the nearest learned counterpart to a fleet.

Synthesis. Chowning's FM synthesis [9] gives the analogy its vocabulary. Horner et al. [10] used genetic algorithms to find FM parameters, a direct precedent for searching a synthesizer's patch space by simulation. This name also comes from there: the DX7 calls each of its operator arrangements an algorithm.

What we believe is different. Not any one layer. It is the whole arrangement: a person writing many interchangeable functions, whole-number selection under a game's Monte Carlo, continuous dials over them, and a learned override that reads the rules' own answer. We call this novel with caution: the search here was brief, and the parts have a long history. A proper literature review would be needed before any such claim in a real publication.

Is there a name? We found no single established name for the exact combination. The closest for the core idea is Programming by Optimization [1]. Hyper-heuristics select among low-level heuristics [13], and neural architecture search chooses among operations, though DARTS relaxes that choice into a blend during search [14], which ours never does. We call the method patch programming and the system Algorhythm. Both are working titles. A web search found no established use of "patch programming" as a method's name, though "patch" usually means a software fix. "Algorhythm" is already in use, by Algorhythm Holdings, a Nasdaq-listed AI company (https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/41050-54), and algorhythms.ai, a generative music tool (https://algorhythms.ai/), among others, so it would need a trademark check, not yet done, before any commercial use. It fits because the Yamaha DX7 calls each of its 32 operator arrangements an algorithm (https://www.righto.com/2021/12/yamaha-dx7-chip-reverse-engineering.html).

The claim, stated precisely. Choosing among implementations by optimization is not itself new. What we did not find is the combination: many hand-written implementations of each decision, whole-number switches and continuous dials optimized together, selection by paired Monte Carlo games, and a learned stage that overrides the rules' answer only when confident.

III. Evolution to This Phase

  1. Fixed rules. A hand-written Commander.
  2. Dials. About 120 numbers it scales by, tuned by a fit over censuses: a census being one team on trial settings against the rest, on paired seeds. Promotion by held-out reads, then scenario families.
  3. Switches. 18 on/off capabilities.
  4. Enumerations. ladder, roles and model, each a whole number choosing a version.
  5. A learned league. Nets in seven slots, and a separate GPU population, crucible.
  6. The verification of 30 October (docs/brain-versions-lab-report.md). No switch could be promoted, owing to two faults; mended, pings off became the first, at +1.99 a seat.
  7. Algorhythm, this paper: functions treated as a patch, with the learned stage placed after the rules rather than instead of them.

IV. The Design

A. The signal path

text
senses + waves ─► DIALS ─► SWITCHES ─► THE RULES ─► TRANSFORM (steps) ─► THE MOVE
  143 + 3         122       6 + 18     one function   confidence-gated

Dials (weights) are continuous: ping_warn_reach, engage_odds. Switches are whole numbers: Settings::choice rounds and clamps, and each site is one match, so exactly one function runs. The rules are deterministic: the same seed plays the same game, so two settings are compared on the same seeds. The transform may then move the answer.

B. How the variables compound

Three kinds of compounding are at work, and they differ in cost.

Multiplicative choice. Each switch multiplies the programs available. Today's brain has 6 choices (8, 4, 5, 5, 8 and 4 versions) and 18 on/off capabilities: 25,600 x 2^18, about 10^9.8 programs before a dial is turned. This cannot be searched exhaustively, and it is not. Each version is read against today's defaults, one at a time.

Dials that mean different things by position. A dial scales the function a switch chose. ping_warn_flee does nothing unless ping_style carries warnings. That is the synthesizer's behaviour: a filter knob is silent if nothing is routed through it. It also has a cost in this method: a dial that does nothing reads as an exact zero, which is indistinguishable from noise to a fit unless flagged. The fleet now flags exact-zero reads.

Interactions found by promotion. A promoted version becomes the baseline, so the next read is conditional on it. A pair that helps only together is invisible to one-at-a-time reads, unless one of the pair happens to get promoted. This is the method's largest blind spot, and precisely what the FM analogy says matters most: some patches are greater than the sum. Wide trials and the transform are partial answers. Explicitly testing pairs of switches is a natural next step.

C. A/B/C/D/E: pings as a worked example

ping_style is a five-way rotary switch over how a team communicates. What Paul asked for, each in the functions, is below. Pings were a single undifferentiated "come here"; now each carries a kind: help, caution, info, warning or defend.

PositionNameCallsComes whenExpect it to work when
A (0)as_builtthe switch pings decides; all are helpas builtthe baseline: the rules as they stand
B (1)helpoutnumbered onlya help call within ping_help_reachcalls are rare and mean something, unlike the old pings
C (2)warnwarnings and cautions, nobody is summoneda warning moves a tank ping_warn_flee away; a caution drops a nearby rallythe army is better spread than massed
D (3)defendonly a base under attacka defence within ping_defend_reach, held ping_defend_hold times as longbases decide games

Each may lose, and that is fine. The earlier read showed that a team with no pings beats one where every ping is a summons. That might mean communication has no value here, or that this one kind of it was bad: help is the test of the second. The dials stay in play: if help wins at a reach of 60, the fit can find whether 40 is better.

Combinations that do not work are expected. warn with a large ping_warn_flee may scatter a team; all with roles = three_guards may leave no one to answer. They run, they read badly, and they are not promoted.

A person is always answered. Whatever the bots do among themselves, a human's ping now works, with the kind the person gives it. Previously, promoting pings off made bots ignore people. A census has no people in it, so this cannot change a census.

D. Intuition: a biorhythm sense

bio is a switch over wave shape: off, sine, triangle, square or saw. Three waves run in the ratio 23:28:33, each seat at its own phase, set in time by bio_period, and they swing engage_odds by bio_amount; the same waves are inputs to the transform.

A note on the name. Biorhythm theory as a claim about people has been reviewed and found unsupported [11], [12]. Nothing here depends on that theory. To a tank this is an LFO: a slow, deterministic oscillator from the synthesizer's own kit, varying how bold each tank is over time. It might help by keeping a team's tanks from all making the same choice at once. The census will say, and it may well say no.

E. The transform, and multi-step

Each Move the rules decide passes through, if patch chooses a slot:

text
x    = senses (143) ; waves (3) ; the rules' answer (direction 2, distance 1)
y[0] = 0
y[k+1] = y[k] + tanh( A[k] [x ; y[k]] + b[k] )
if y[n].confidence >= patch_confidence:  move += (y[n].across, y[n].down) * patch_reach

Multi-step means each step reads the steps before: one may propose and the next correct, a chain rather than one matrix, much as operators in FM feed each other. At all zeros, nothing changes, which is the property taken from residual learning [4], [5]: training starts where the rules are. And it is a gate, not a blend: the transform stays silent until confident, which is "up to the AI net to override if its confident".

Only the chosen one runs. At patch = 0 no senses are read for it and no time is spent. The same goes for each ping style and each wave.

What reads what. The learned nets in model replace the rules entirely, and every slot loses by about 20 a seat. The transform inherits the rules' strength. We expect that to matter, and it is not yet shown.

The trainer is SPSA over the transform's 918 numbers, because a step costs one paired census however many numbers there are. Each step is noisy, so a step's own reading is not the judge: the finished transform goes in a slot and is read like any version. crucible cannot train it, because crucible's arena has its own senses and net.

VI. Results

The current brains are intact. With all new layers at their defaults, the nine scenario families read exactly as before the change, number for number (319/400, 400/400, 326/400, 400/400, 998/1000, 993/1000, 1000/1000, 997/1000, 812/1000). All treadcore tests pass, with 12 new ones in the brain: each kind does what it says; a style hears only what it carries; a person is answered; a zero patch changes nothing; an unconfident patch changes nothing and a confident one does; a later step reads an earlier one.

The new switches are alive. A smoke test on one Mac, 9 seeds, 900-second games on three maps, against the defaults:

These show that games change, and no more: the fleet's own first read is 55 seeds, and its bar is two held-out reads of 400. The naval figure is three seeds. It is worth reading properly, not worth believing yet.

A trap we fell into. A first smoke test used 300-second games on one map, and even engage_odds read exactly zero: in five minutes those tanks barely fight. A switch must be judged by full-length games, and an exact zero on a short test means little.

Nothing here is a measurement of strength yet. We do not know that typed pings, intuition or a transform are better than what they extend.

A first test of confident overrides

crucible is a separate, fast GPU arena with simple tanks. It was given the same structure: a scripted tank decides, a net reads the senses and that answer and gives a correction and a confidence, and below zero the gate stays shut. With its gates almost always shut, the hybrid ties the rules, as designed; it then gains while overriding on few steps. The rows to 299 are one 300-generation run of each kind; the later rows are a separate 3,000-generation hybrid run on another seed.

Limits. One simple arena and one fixed opponent, which is the very rule the hybrid builds on, so some of the gain may exploit that rule. Wins level off near 60% from about generation 1,000. Plain islands did eventually reach about 95% against this opponent after over 100,000 generations, so the advantage shown is mostly how fast the hybrid starts, and where it ends is not shown. Its nets cannot be moved to treadcore, whose senses differ, and its transform is one step. This supports the design. It does not show that a transform will help in treadcore.

VII. Discussion

Limitations. One-at-a-time reads miss the interactions the analogy prizes most. A 10^9.8 space is sampled very sparsely. A census of bots cannot value what a person wants, as pings showed. Every switch position costs seeds, and most positions so far have read as nothing. And the transform is exposed to overfitting a score, where a person's idea of good play may be different.

Future work. Train a first transform; read ping_style and bio on the fleet; find why ladder = hunt_first plays identical games; test pairs of switches; per-seat or per-role patches, so that each tank in a team truly takes its own path through the code; rewritten functions such as target_pick; connect crucible.

VIII. Conclusion

Algorhythm treats a game agent as a patch: many functions by hand, dials and switches set by simulation, a learned stage that may override when it is sure. The structure is in place and proven silent. What it can actually achieve remains to be measured.
